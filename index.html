Chapter Twelve

Lots of Me

Chapter Thirteen

Crack in Reality

Chapter Fifteen

Restoration

Chapter Sixteen

Internal Conflict: Remaining Morality

He looked at that face for a long moment.

He wanted to go home. He let himself want it fully, without managing it. The specific dinner table. The specific hum. He wanted it and knew he wasn’t going to have it and sat with both of those things at the same time until the wanting didn’t feel like an argument anymore. Just a fact. Just something true that didn’t change the other true thing.

He stood up. His legs were unsteady. That was fine.

The mechanics were simple, in the end — the necklace had shown him once, one vision different from all the others: a figure stepping not through a crack but into the membrane between, into the place where the seal needed to be. Not crossing. Becoming the closure. He had understood it immediately and closed the vision and not opened it again until now.

He walked to the edge of the fountain. He didn’t look at the scared version of himself on the other side. There was nothing to say to that face. You’re going to be okay was probably not true. The long way was the only way. Some things you had to find out by living them and there was no shortcut he could hand across the gap.

The necklace burned. He kept walking.

Twice he nearly stopped. Once at the very edge, one foot still on the ground, the other hovering, his body making a last unilateral argument for the dinner table and the coast road and the seventy percent and whatever percentage came after, if he stayed, if he kept going, if he just —

He stepped in.

From outside: a person at a fountain, hand closed around something small, one foot lifted. Then no person. No transition. No light or sound or theatre. Just the bench, and the water, and an absence shaped exactly like someone who had decided, after everything, that the only thing he had ever truly owned was the choice of what to do with himself at the end.

The crack sealed. The others followed — across the city, across the weeks — dimming, closing, the air going back to the ordinary quality of air that does not have something wrong with it. The world healed without knowing what it was healing from. Without ceremony. Without anyone left to be thanked or blamed or remembered by name.

———

There is no ceiling here.

No room. No morning light. No hum from downstairs.

No father behind a newspaper. No mother who smiles the real smile.

No old friends who wave in hallways. No bench. No fountain. No crack above the water.

No other version of this face anywhere in any world.

No world.

No other.

Just this. Just here. Just the particular silence of a space with nothing in it except the one who chose to be here instead.

I wanted a better life.

I got one.

And then I did the only thing I had left that was actually mine to give.

Only me. Finally, completely, at a cost I understood — only me.
